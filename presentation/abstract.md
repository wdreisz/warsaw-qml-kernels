# Abstract: a fair reality check for quantum-kernel SVMs

Quantum-kernel SVMs are often shown working on small tabular datasets without a tuned classical baseline. We rerun that comparison fairly. A fidelity kernel from Qiskit's ZZ feature map (2–12 qubits) competes with an RBF SVM and logistic regression on identical PCA features and identical repeated stratified CV splits, with nested CV for every hyperparameter and Nadeau–Bengio corrected t-tests, on Wisconsin breast cancer and wine. With its bandwidth tuned, the quantum kernel beats the RBF SVM in 0 of 12 settings (−1.9 pts to −0.4 pts). At the tutorial scaling, kernel values concentrate and breast-cancer accuracy falls from 89.5% to 66.0%. Cross-validation picks a small bandwidth (median 0.05), where the geometric difference to an RBF kernel is only 1.2–3.1. A FakeTorino noise model distorts kernel entries by RMSE 0.27–0.31, against 0.010–0.012 from 1,000 shots. Everything is classical simulation; we claim no quantum advantage.
